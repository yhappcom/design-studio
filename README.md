# Design Studio

A professional design research and practice repository for building reusable expertise across product design, visual design, typography, type design, interaction design, accessibility, and design engineering collaboration.

This repository does **not** define one reusable visual style for every product. Its purpose is to develop a reusable **method of thinking, researching, composing, critiquing, validating, and collaborating** so that each product can arrive at its own appropriate visual language.

## Studio mandate

Design Studio participates in product work as an independent design discipline.

The default sequence is:

**understand → research → frame → compose → prototype → critique → validate → reconcile with engineering → refine**

Implementation constraints are considered seriously, but they do not pre-emptively flatten design exploration. Product semantics and user needs come first; design and engineering reconcile after a strong design proposition exists.

## Core disciplines

- Typography and type design
- Visual composition and graphic systems
- Information design and hierarchy
- Interaction and product design
- Accessibility and human factors
- Responsive/adaptive design
- Design research and precedent analysis
- Critique and design authorship
- Design-to-engineering handoff and validation

## Repository structure

- `curriculum/` — structured study from foundations through advanced practice
- `research/` — source-based studies and literature notes
- `methods/` — reusable design methods, critique frameworks, and gates
- `type-design/` — professional type-design curriculum, exercises, and production knowledge
- `product-design/` — product/interaction/visual-system curriculum and exercises
- `case-studies/` — product-specific applications; these do not become universal style rules
- `progress/` — current learning status, gates, and completion evidence

## Professional standard

A topic is not considered learned because it has been read once. Completion requires:

1. authoritative-source study;
2. terminology and principle mastery;
3. practical exercises;
4. critique against explicit criteria;
5. application in more than one context;
6. failure analysis;
7. evidence that the method survives accessibility and technical constraints;
8. written synthesis that can guide future projects.

The goal is not to accumulate references. The goal is to develop judgment.

## First case study

LogMate is the first major case study because it exposes demanding problems in typography, dense data, mobile/landscape composition, accessibility, and premium product identity. LogMate-specific decisions remain inside its case study and its own repository; Design Studio extracts only transferable knowledge.
