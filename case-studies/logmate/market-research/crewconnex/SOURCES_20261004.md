## Source-use rules

1. **Operator/PDC/government sources outrank aggregators** when they measure the same thing at a similar date.
2. Fleet-tracking databases are used when the operator does not publish counts; their scope is recorded explicitly.
3. A source that says an airline uses PDC is not sufficient to label it CrewConnex.
4. A dated pilot count is never silently promoted to a 2026 count.
5. Union membership is a lower-bound/partial population unless union coverage is proven complete.
6. Model outputs must never overwrite raw observations.

## Known source conflicts / caveats

- **Air Alsie:** the official operator describes a broad managed/operated fleet; ownership/tracking databases show a smaller subset. The base CSV uses the narrower 8-aircraft comparable count and flags the scope mismatch.
- **Arkia:** current trackers include wet-leased/external registrations. Fleet count is operational footprint, not necessarily owned fleet.
- **Emerald:** Ireland and UK AOCs are separated in aircraft databases; the market model uses the combined group footprint because the product opportunity is crew-based.
- **West Atlantic:** the public brand describes a much larger overall fleet/enterprise footprint, while current UK/Sweden AOC tracker records sum to 11. The initial model deliberately uses the traceable AOC subset.
- **PDC customer list:** current and useful for corroboration, but its products are modular; it cannot identify CrewConnex use by itself.
