## SYNTHESIS
### IP-T1 — Typography follows semantic role, not brand uniformity
Use proportional UI typography for general interface roles and a mature exact mono role only where the product contract requires operational comparison. Do not blanket-mono the application.

### IP-T2 — Stable comparison is a Type + Layout contract
Mono advance can stabilize characters but cannot by itself stabilize mixed Flight identifiers. Preserve canonical carrier/number-suffix zoning and shared semantic column boundaries.

### IP-T3 — Production geometry must be based on mature shipping candidates
Until T021 drawing and later spacing gates close, the custom research font cannot set LogMate production widths, density or breakpoints. Mature production/fallback controls are the geometry authority.

### IP-T4 — Text semantics outrank fit pressure
Do not solve narrow layouts by negative tracking, shrinking required text, ad-hoc abbreviation or premature kerning. Recompose/scroll/wrap according to the surface contract.

### IP-T5 — English-only product UI does not remove Unicode/source-data stress
Canonical authored UI is English-only. Imported/user evidence, identifiers and free text still require fallback-safe Unicode handling; numeric date locale ordering remains a separate presentation stressor.

## PRACTICE / CRITIQUE
Applied the principles to Home summary metrics, View Logbook ledger, Activity period labels, Add Flight fields and import/mapping rows. No principle requires the custom font; all survive a mature proportional + exact mature mono pairing. The most fragile surface remains the dense ledger under large text and locale-date width.

Do not promote on static appearance alone; follow W103 for runtime transfer.
