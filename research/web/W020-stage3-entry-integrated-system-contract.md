## Form-factor transfer contract
Mobile web: serialized list/detail is acceptable if route/history/context survive. Tablet web: master/detail may be simultaneous. Desktop web: dense list/table/detail may coexist. Breakpoints are consequences of content/behavior fit, not device-name constants.

DOM/source order must remain meaningful when visual layout changes. Hidden/inert/transient layers must not steal or obscure focus. Sticky content must be tested against WCAG 2.2 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum). Target Size (Minimum) remains a web conformance constraint with its defined exceptions, not a complete touch-UX quality metric. W3C's published WCAG 2.2 remains the conformance baseline; supporting techniques are informative rather than the normative success criteria.

## Runtime transfer plan
Highest-value executable specimen is one integrated page/system, not separate demos. It should test together:
1. responsive phone/tablet/desktop composition;
2. keyboard reading/focus order;
3. sticky/transient focus-not-obscured behavior;
4. semantic status exposure for pending/confirmed/failure/outcome-unknown;
5. pseudo-localized long strings and 200% text/zoom stress;
6. light/dark/forced-colors token survival;
7. reduced-motion equivalence;
8. route/history and Fetch known-vs-unknown outcome when environment policy permits.

Blocked HTTP/network remains an environment blocker, not product failure. A future executable run must state whether it used real HTTP, same-document surrogate or deterministic model.

## Performance evidence boundary
Design may reserve geometry and reduce unnecessary work, but local/headless timings and synthetic layout-shift observations are lab diagnostics. Production LCP/INP/CLS claims require field-capable evidence and are kept separate.
