# Master Curriculum

Status: ACTIVE

The studio curriculum is designed to build professional judgment, not just familiarity with terminology. Reading alone does not complete a module.

The curriculum applies across the current specialist roles — Typography / Type Design, Color, and Layout / Spatial / Interaction — with domain-specific evidence requirements. Future specialists inherit the same progression unless a justified domain-specific adaptation is approved.

## Stage 1 — Foundations

### Visual foundations
- figure/ground, gestalt, visual mass, balance, tension
- proportion, scale, rhythm, repetition, contrast
- negative space and edge relationships
- grid systems and intentional grid-breaking
- color perception, luminance, simultaneous contrast
- typography as composition and information architecture
- design history and precedent literacy

### Type foundations
- character/glyph/font distinctions
- anatomy, metrics, UPM, alignment zones
- stroke/contrast models and historical construction logic
- Bézier drawing discipline
- overshoot and optical correction
- spacing before kerning
- numeral systems and punctuation
- proofing at intended size

### Interaction foundations
- affordance, feedback, mapping, consistency
- recognition vs recall
- task/object/action relationships
- navigation models
- system status and error prevention
- reversibility, modes and recovery
- accessibility as a design constraint

**Gate:** explain and demonstrate each principle using original exercises, not only definitions; show that relevant peer-domain evidence was checked and reused rather than duplicated.

## Stage 2 — Intermediate professional practice

### Information and product design
- task analysis and primary-question framing
- information hierarchy and IA
- dense-data vs low-density composition
- forms, tables, search, settings, empty/error/loading states
- responsive and adaptive composition
- typography systems across multiple roles
- iconography and non-text signals
- component systems without component-driven sameness
- interaction states, async behavior and recovery paths

### Type design
- coherent glyph families
- spacing systems and control strings
- kerning classes and exceptions
- figure styles: proportional/tabular, lining/oldstyle where relevant
- diacritics and punctuation systems
- weight/width relationships
- interpolation fundamentals
- screen rendering and small-size compensation

### Color systems
- palette/ramp construction with explicit authoring models
- semantic color-role systems
- state/focus color coupled to interaction semantics
- gamut-aware production values and fallback behavior
- viewing-condition and device-aware validation

### Research and critique
- precedent analysis without imitation
- comparative studies
- hypothesis and experiment design
- critique vocabulary
- KEEP / REWORK / REJECT rationale
- identifying genericness, novelty-for-novelty and implementation bias
- cross-specialist dependency and handoff discipline

**Gate:** produce multiple solutions to the same problem and defend the selected direction using explicit criteria, including evidence borrowed correctly from adjacent specialties.

## Stage 3 — Advanced / systems practice

### Visual and interaction systems
- brand expression through structure rather than decoration
- multi-surface identity: phone/tablet/desktop/web/print
- editorial systems
- motion hierarchy and reduced-motion equivalence
- complex tables and long-form data
- mixed-script typography and fallback behavior
- design tokens as semantic systems
- navigation/state systems across multiple form factors
- failure, interruption and recovery across surfaces

### Advanced type
- family planning and character-set strategy
- master compatibility and interpolation quality
- optical-size thinking
- OpenType layout: GSUB/GPOS/GDEF literacy
- variable-font architecture and axis discipline
- vertical metrics and cross-platform behavior
- hinting/rasterization concepts
- licensing, naming and font IP hygiene

### Advanced color
- cross-gamut design systems
- ICC/color-management literacy
- perceptual model limits and color-difference interpretation
- environment/display validation
- data-visualization palette systems
- dark/light/adaptive color behavior

### Human factors and accessibility
- contrast and glare
- text scaling/reflow
- motor target constraints
- focus visibility and keyboard paths
- color-vision independence
- cognitive load and scan behavior
- data legibility under stress
- multi-modal equivalence and recovery

**Gate:** demonstrate one coherent system across at least three form factors and adverse/accessibility states without losing identity, usability or evidence traceability.

## Stage 4 — Production and authorship

### Design production
- reproducible design evidence
- source control and versioning
- structured critique history
- handoff specifications
- implementation negotiation
- device validation
- discrepancy tracking between design and implementation
- cross-specialist integration records

### Font production
- reproducible source/build pipeline
- fontTools/fontmake literacy
- automated QA and fontbakery-style checks
- naming/versioning
- proof generation
- platform/browser/device comparison
- no undocumented post-export fixes

### Color production
- reproducible color-token generation and conversion
- production-space encoding and fallback documentation
- managed vs unmanaged rendering awareness
- device/browser/profile validation
- no undocumented manual color corrections

### Interaction production
- explicit state models
- reproducible prototypes
- keyboard/touch/pointer behavior validation
- async/failure/recovery scenarios
- accessibility and assistive-technology evidence

### Authorship
- articulate a design thesis
- distinguish reference influence from imitation
- create a coherent body of work
- explain why each formal decision exists
- document failures and course corrections
- demonstrate that quality survives removal of fashionable effects

**Gate:** complete an end-to-end case study with research, alternatives, critique, production constraints, accessibility evidence, peer-domain reuse and final rationale.

## Stage 5 — Research and advisory

This is the target stage for specialists expected to support publication-quality argument and enterprise-level consulting.

### Research literacy
- systematic literature search and source-quality assessment
- distinguish primary evidence, secondary synthesis, normative standards and professional convention
- research-question and hypothesis formulation
- experimental and observational design
- sampling, measurement and operationalization literacy
- statistical literacy sufficient to interpret design/HCI/perception research critically
- effect-size and uncertainty awareness
- replication, external-validity and ecological-validity critique
- competing-evidence synthesis
- explicit limitation and uncertainty reporting

### Independent research
- identify `Known → Unknown → Gap → Research Question`
- design reproducible studies appropriate to the domain
- predefine variables, controls, evaluation criteria and failure conditions when applicable
- separate model/calculation evidence from rendered, behavioral, device and human-observation evidence
- produce research reports with Abstract / Introduction / Literature Review / Method / Results / Discussion / Limitations / Conclusion / References as appropriate
- defend claims against expert critique and revise them when evidence fails

### Enterprise advisory
- problem framing under ambiguous organizational constraints
- stakeholder and system-boundary analysis
- identify competing objectives: brand, usability, accessibility, engineering, localization, performance, legal/standards and operations
- create multiple viable options rather than presenting taste as a single answer
- analyze trade-offs, risks, reversibility, cost of change and governance burden
- distinguish global rules from local exceptions
- advise on multi-product / multi-platform design-system decisions
- explain recommendations at executive, design and engineering levels without changing the underlying evidence
- explicitly identify what remains unknown and what evidence would change the recommendation

### Cross-specialist synthesis
- integrate findings from adjacent specialists without duplicating their research
- recognize when a recommendation depends on Type, Color, Layout/Interaction or a future specialist
- request and use canonical peer evidence
- document disagreements and unresolved evidence rather than forcing consensus
- produce joint recommendations with traceable ownership of claims

### Scholarly / advisory authorship
- write a defensible long-form thesis or paper-like artifact grounded in primary evidence
- conduct a structured defense against counterarguments
- produce an enterprise advisory memo with options, evidence, trade-offs, risks and recommendation
- distinguish universal evidence from product-specific judgment
- demonstrate responsible uncertainty

**Gate:** complete both:

1. a research-grade domain study or thesis with reproducible method, critical literature review, explicit limitations and defensible conclusions; and
2. an enterprise-scale advisory case in which multiple options are analyzed, cross-specialist evidence is integrated, risks/trade-offs are explicit, and recommendations survive structured critique.

Stage 5 does not mean the specialist is infallible or that learning stops. It means the specialist can operate responsibly at research/advisory level with traceable evidence and uncertainty.

## Cross-cutting study areas

- typography history
- graphic-design history
- industrial/product design precedents
- editorial design
- information visualization
- perception and cognition
- accessibility standards
- platform HIG/material guidance as evidence, not stylistic law
- research methodology and statistics
- front-end implementation literacy sufficient for credible negotiation
- organizational design-system governance

## Completion

The curriculum is complete only when `progress/STATUS.md` and the specialist status files mark the required gates `PASS` with linked evidence.

`READ`, `NOTED`, file count, or confident explanation do not equal PASS.

A completed specialist must also demonstrate that it can reuse peer-domain evidence, identify uncertainty, accept contradictory evidence, and revise recommendations.
