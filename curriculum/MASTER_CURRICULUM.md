# Master Curriculum

Status: ACTIVE

The studio curriculum is designed to build professional judgment, not just familiarity with terminology. Reading alone does not complete a module.

## Stage 1 — Foundations

### Visual foundations
- figure/ground, gestalt, visual mass, balance, tension
- proportion, scale, rhythm, repetition, contrast
- negative space and edge relationships
- grid systems and intentional grid-breaking
- color perception, luminance, simultaneous contrast
- typography as composition and information architecture
- design history and precedent literacy

### Type foundations
- character/glyph/font distinctions
- anatomy, metrics, UPM, alignment zones
- stroke/contrast models and historical construction logic
- Bézier drawing discipline
- overshoot and optical correction
- spacing before kerning
- numeral systems and punctuation
- proofing at intended size

### Interaction foundations
- affordance, feedback, mapping, consistency
- recognition vs recall
- task/object/action relationships
- navigation models
- system status and error prevention
- accessibility as a design constraint

**Gate:** explain and demonstrate each principle using original exercises, not only definitions.

## Stage 2 — Intermediate professional practice

### Information and product design
- task analysis and primary-question framing
- information hierarchy and IA
- dense-data vs low-density composition
- forms, tables, search, settings, empty/error/loading states
- responsive and adaptive composition
- typography systems across multiple roles
- iconography and non-text signals
- component systems without component-driven sameness

### Type design
- coherent glyph families
- spacing systems and control strings
- kerning classes and exceptions
- figure styles: proportional/tabular, lining/oldstyle where relevant
- diacritics and punctuation systems
- weight/width relationships
- interpolation fundamentals
- screen rendering and small-size compensation

### Research and critique
- precedent analysis without imitation
- comparative studies
- hypothesis and experiment design
- critique vocabulary
- KEEP / REWORK / REJECT rationale
- identifying genericness, novelty-for-novelty, and implementation bias

**Gate:** produce multiple solutions to the same problem and defend the selected direction using explicit criteria.

## Stage 3 — Advanced / systems practice

### Visual systems and identity
- brand expression through structure rather than decoration
- multi-surface identity: phone/tablet/desktop/web/print
- editorial systems
- motion hierarchy and reduced-motion equivalence
- complex tables and long-form data
- mixed-script typography and fallback behavior
- design tokens as semantic systems

### Advanced type
- family planning and character-set strategy
- master compatibility and interpolation quality
- optical-size thinking
- OpenType layout: GSUB/GPOS/GDEF literacy
- variable-font architecture and axis discipline
- vertical metrics and cross-platform behavior
- hinting/rasterization concepts
- licensing, naming, and font IP hygiene

### Human factors and accessibility
- contrast and glare
- text scaling/reflow
- motor target constraints
- focus visibility and keyboard paths
- color-vision independence
- cognitive load and scan behavior
- data legibility under stress

**Gate:** demonstrate one coherent system across at least three form factors and adverse/accessibility states without losing identity.

## Stage 4 — Production and authorship

### Design production
- reproducible design evidence
- source control and versioning
- structured critique history
- handoff specifications
- implementation negotiation
- device validation
- discrepancy tracking between design and implementation

### Font production
- reproducible source/build pipeline
- fontTools/fontmake literacy
- automated QA and fontbakery-style checks
- naming/versioning
- proof generation
- platform/browser/device comparison
- no undocumented post-export fixes

### Authorship
- articulate a design thesis
- distinguish reference influence from imitation
- create a coherent body of work
- explain why each formal decision exists
- document failures and course corrections
- demonstrate that quality survives removal of fashionable effects

**Gate:** complete an end-to-end case study with research, alternatives, critique, production constraints, accessibility evidence, and final rationale.

## Cross-cutting study areas

- typography history
- graphic-design history
- industrial/product design precedents
- editorial design
- information visualization
- perception and cognition
- accessibility standards
- platform HIG/material guidance as evidence, not stylistic law
- front-end implementation literacy sufficient for credible negotiation

## Completion

The curriculum is complete only when `progress/STATUS.md` marks every gate PASS with links to evidence. `READ` or `NOTED` does not equal PASS.
