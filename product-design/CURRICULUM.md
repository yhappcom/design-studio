# Graduate-Level Product / Visual Design Curriculum

The aim is professional judgment across product, interaction, information, visual systems, and implementation collaboration.

## Foundation

- composition and figure/ground
- balance, tension, scale, visual mass
- rhythm, repetition, contrast
- negative space
- grid systems
- typography as information structure
- color/luminance and perceptual contrast
- iconography and non-text signals
- design history and precedent literacy

## Intermediate

- task and workflow analysis
- information architecture
- object/action/destination separation
- hierarchy for dense and sparse information
- forms, tables, search, settings, navigation
- state design: empty, loading, error, success, disabled, focused
- responsive/adaptive composition
- content strategy and microcopy relationships
- component systems without visual homogenization

## Advanced

- complex cross-platform systems
- brand expression through structure
- editorial systems inside software
- mixed density and long-form data
- motion hierarchy and reduced-motion equivalents
- mixed-script typography
- design tokens as semantic systems
- accessibility/human-factors integration
- research-backed design strategy
- critique and authorship

## Research method

For each significant design problem:

1. establish known facts and unknowns;
2. identify the user's actual question/task;
3. gather primary evidence and precedents;
4. extract principles rather than motifs;
5. generate materially different hypotheses;
6. prototype at realistic content density;
7. critique against explicit criteria;
8. test stress/accessibility cases;
9. document why one direction survives.

## Professional gate

A screen is not successful because it is polished. It must be clear, specific to its product, resilient under stress, accessible, and difficult to improve without changing the underlying premise.

A system is not successful because every screen looks identical. It succeeds when different screens feel related while preserving the correct hierarchy for each task.
